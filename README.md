# Patient Analysis
## Overview
This analysis uses box plots to analyse cholesterol levels across different smoker categories and compare systolic blood pressure among patients with and without hypertension.

## Objectives
- Compare cholesterol levels across smoker statuses.
- Identify extreme cholesterol outliers using the 1.5 × IQR rule.
- Compare systolic blood pressure distributions between patients diagnosed and not diagnosed with hypertension.
- Understand how box plots help identify patterns and differences in patient data.

## Analysis Performed
### 1. Cholesterol by Smoker Status
A box plot was used to compare cholesterol levels among non-smokers, former smokers and current smokers. The analysis examined the median, upper whisker and extreme outliers.

### 2. Systolic Blood Pressure by Hypertension Diagnosis
Side-by-side box plots were constructed to compare systolic blood pressure among patients with and without hypertension. The overlap and separation between the distributions were used to assess differences in blood pressure.

## Key Findings
- Cholesterol distributions were relatively similar across smoker categories.
- Non-smokers had the highest number of upper extreme outliers.
- Patients diagnosed with hypertension had a higher median systolic blood pressure than those without a diagnosis.
- The separation between the systolic blood pressure distributions suggests that blood pressure is associated with hypertension diagnosis.

## Conclusion
This analysis provides a visual way to compare patient groups, examine distribution patterns and identify extreme values. The analysis highlighted differences in systolic blood pressure between patients with and without hypertension.
